Do theatrical adaptations of stage musicals make money?

A practical introduction to using comparable past cases when evaluating a new proposal—and to keeping the resulting evidence honest, inspectable, and useful.

The basic idea. When someone proposes a new project, the plan is usually built from details unique to that project: the team, the creative vision, the market opportunity, and the operating model. Flyvbjerg complements that inside view with a simpler question: what happened when other people attempted genuinely comparable things?

What Flyvbjerg adds

A spreadsheet can calculate a median. The harder problem is deciding which historical cases belong in the comparison, finding the same measurement for each case, preserving disagreements and missing values, and showing which conclusions change when reasonable assumptions change.

A disciplined comparison

Define what counts as comparable before looking at success or failure. This prevents a memorable hit, notorious failure, or convenient data source from quietly determining the sample.

Evidence with a memory

Keep the source, candidate value, review decision, missing-data state, and metric definition attached to every case. A later correction does not erase what was previously believed.

An honest outside view

Summarize the full historical distribution rather than selecting a flattering average. Preserve ranges, skew, downside frequency, and dependence among related cases.

Conclusions that can be challenged

Compare reasonable analytical choices and identify exactly which cases switch classifications. Decision-makers can see what is robust and what depends on an assumption.

This tutorial follows one question from beginning to end: if a producer is considering another film adaptation of a stage musical, what should the history of similar films contribute to the greenlight decision?

1Why use an outside view?

Imagine a producer considering a film version of a successful stage musical. The proposal may emphasize a known title, proven songs, awards, stars, and an existing audience. Those details matter—but almost every proposal has a persuasive story about why this case is special.

An outside view begins elsewhere. It identifies past projects that faced the same basic decision, records what happened across the whole group, and asks where the new proposal sits in that distribution. It does not replace project-specific judgment. It supplies a historical baseline that the project-specific story must explain and, when appropriate, overcome.

Decision target. What base-rate range and downside allowance should a producer or financier use for a new theatrically released adaptation of a stage musical?

Here, one comparable case is one film adaptation. The common outcome is worldwide theatrical gross relative to reported production budget. That measurement is available for many films and reveals downside and upside—but it is not the same thing as studio profit. A useful system should help maintain that distinction rather than bury it.

What the package does: it gives a coding agent a structured place to assemble comparable cases, record evidence, make review decisions, preserve unknowns, freeze a historical distribution, and test whether the decision changes under reasonable alternative assumptions.

2Describe the decision before gathering evidence

Start by telling Flyvbjerg what decision the historical evidence is meant to inform. This keeps the research anchored to a real choice rather than becoming a collection of interesting film facts.

mkdir theatrical-musicals
cd theatrical-musicals
flyvbjerg init

cat > target.json <<'JSON'
{
  "decision": "What base-rate range and downside allowance should a producer or financier use for a new theatrically released stage-musical adaptation?",
  "thresholds_gross_multiple": [1.0, 2.0, 2.5],
  "unknown": [
    "production budget range",
    "marketing spend",

    "participations",

flyvbjerg target create theatrical_musical \

3Decide what counts as comparable

Include English-language feature films directly adapted from a previously produced stage musical, released theatrically in the United States from 2006 through 2025 by an established distributor. Exclude original screen musicals, animation, concert and television films, streaming-first releases, and sequels that do not directly adapt the stage work.

Included

Hairspray, Les Misérables, Cats, In the Heights, Wicked, and other films satisfying stage provenance and release rules.

Excluded

La La Land is an original screen musical. Mamma Mia! Here We Go Again is a sequel rather than a direct adaptation of the stage book.

Keep a record of every title considered, including exclusions. Here we considered 20 candidates before collecting financial results: 18 qualified and two did not. La La Land remains in the audit trail with the reason it was excluded; it does not simply disappear. Notice that the excluded record needs no budget or box-office result—the decision is based only on whether the film fits the definition.

flyvbjerg intake add theatrical_musicals wiki_la_la_land \
  membership_candidate \
  "Original screen musical rather than an adaptation" \
  --proposed-name "La La Land"
See the generated intake identifier
{
  "item_id": "item_7b8f...",
  "kind": "membership_candidate",
  "proposed_names": ["La La Land"],
  "status": "untriaged"
}

Copy the returned item_id into the decision command. In the command below, item_7b8f... means the identifier emitted immediately above:

flyvbjerg intake defer theatrical_musicals item_7b8f... \
  --reason original_screen_musical
flyvbjerg next
Do not require observed outcomes for membership. If an eligible film lacks a budget or gross, retain its case and mark that measurement missing. Otherwise data availability becomes a hidden selection rule.

4Find the comparable cases and their histories

A coding agent searches and reads Wikipedia film histories using its research tools. Flyvbjerg is the evidence ledger, not the researcher: it does not follow URLs or silently convert whatever a search happens to return into data.

Agent actionFlyvbjerg action
Find candidate adaptationsNone
Check stage provenance and US theatrical releaseStore a case identity
Read budget bounds and worldwide grossStore candidates linked to the source
Find conflict or omissionKeep unresolved or record an absence state
Calculate gross multiplesPreserve candidates and evidence decisions

Wikipedia is good best-available evidence for this first pass. A high-stakes investment should verify contested financial values with studio records, trade reporting, and commercial box-office data.

7Use the same measurements across cases

Five versioned metrics preserve reported ranges instead of inventing midpoints:

budget_low_musd

Lower production-budget bound, nominal $m.

budget_high_musd

Upper production-budget bound, nominal $m.

worldwide_gross_musd

Reported worldwide theatrical gross, nominal $m.

gross_multiple_low

Conservative multiple: gross ÷ high budget.

gross_multiple_high

Optimistic multiple: gross ÷ low budget.

Construct all five definitions directly. Nothing in this block depends on a pre-existing file:

flyvbjerg metric add theatrical_musicals budget_low_musd \
  --kind numeric --role input --unit million_usd --subject-kind case \
  --label "Reported production budget lower bound" \
  --definition "Lower reported production-budget bound, nominal USD millions." \
  --zero-policy "Missing or malformed budget data remain missing and are never zero."

flyvbjerg metric add theatrical_musicals budget_high_musd \
  --kind numeric --role input --unit million_usd --subject-kind case \
  --label "Reported production budget upper bound" \
  --definition "Upper reported production-budget bound, nominal USD millions." \
  --zero-policy "Missing or malformed budget data remain missing and are never zero."

flyvbjerg metric add theatrical_musicals worldwide_gross_musd \
  --kind numeric --role outcome --unit million_usd --subject-kind case \
  --label "Worldwide theatrical box-office gross" \
  --definition "Reported worldwide theatrical gross, nominal USD millions." \
  --zero-policy "Missing or malformed box-office data remain missing and are never zero."

flyvbjerg metric add theatrical_musicals gross_multiple_low \
  --kind ratio --role outcome --unit x --subject-kind case \
  --label "Conservative worldwide gross multiple" \
  --definition "Worldwide gross divided by the upper production-budget bound." \
  --zero-policy "Missing inputs remain missing; a failed calculation is never zero."

flyvbjerg metric add theatrical_musicals gross_multiple_high \
  --kind ratio --role outcome --unit x --subject-kind case \
  --label "Optimistic worldwide gross multiple" \
  --definition "Worldwide gross divided by the lower production-budget bound." \
  --zero-policy "Missing inputs remain missing; a failed calculation is never zero."

flyvbjerg next
Zero policy. Missing or malformed inputs remain missing. A failed lookup or division is never recorded as zero gross or a negative outcome.

8Record possible values without treating them as truth

Cats has an $80–100m reported budget and $75.5m worldwide gross. Store the range and both resulting multiples:

flyvbjerg observation add theatrical_musicals \
  --subject-kind case --subject cats --metric budget_low_musd \
  --value 80 --source wiki_cats --period 2019 --method reported
flyvbjerg observation add theatrical_musicals \
  --subject-kind case --subject cats --metric budget_high_musd \
  --value 100 --source wiki_cats --period 2019 --method reported
flyvbjerg observation add theatrical_musicals \
  --subject-kind case --subject cats --metric worldwide_gross_musd \
  --value 75.5 --source wiki_cats --period 2019 --method reported
flyvbjerg observation add theatrical_musicals \
  --subject-kind case --subject cats --metric gross_multiple_low \
  --value 0.755 --source wiki_cats --period 2019 --method calculated
flyvbjerg observation add theatrical_musicals \
  --subject-kind case --subject cats --metric gross_multiple_high \

10Preserve what is missing or unknown

flyvbjerg coverage set theatrical_musicals \
  --subject-kind case --subject cats --metric gross_multiple_low \
  --state observed \
  --reason "Accepted multiple with complete reported inputs."

All 18 seed films have observed ratio bounds. That does not prove every eligible film would. An expanded enumeration may produce not_found, conflicted, or other explicit absence states.

Complete measurement is not complete discovery. The denominator is 18 observed seed cases, not a claimed census of all qualifying releases.

12Stress-test the decision conclusion

Below 1.0x is a strong theatrical downside signal. From 1.0x to under 2.5x is economically indeterminate. At least 2.5x is a strong theatrical-recovery proxy—not audited profit.

flyvbjerg threshold analysis_bca59aad72a74ac3 \
  --operator lt --value 1.0
flyvbjerg threshold analysis_bca59aad72a74ac3 \
  --operator ge --value 2.5
flyvbjerg locate analysis_bca59aad72a74ac3 \
  --value 2.5 --label "Strong theatrical-recovery proxy"
ZoneObserved filmsMeaning
Below 1.0x8/18 (44.4%)Gross did not match production budget.
1.0x–2.5x3/18 (16.7%)Profit unresolved by common fields.
At least 2.5x7/18 (38.9%)Strong theatrical recovery proxy.

At a looser 2.0x boundary, budget ranges move the count from 7/18 to 9/18. At 1.0x and 2.5x, the classifications are unchanged by budget uncertainty.

14Reproduce and audit the work

The analytical story above is the user-facing result. Underneath it, Flyvbjerg preserves enough structure to answer practical challenges: Which source supported this value? Was it reviewed? Which films were excluded, and why? Did a corrected budget change the conclusion?

The checked-in cohort.json is the canonical input for this worked example. A maintainer or skeptical reviewer can reconstruct the entire workspace from the root of a Flyvbjerg repository checkout. This is a separate location from the temporary theatrical-musicals workspace created in chapter 2:

cd /path/to/flyvbjerg
python simulations/theatrical-musicals/rebuild.py \
  /tmp/theatrical-musicals-audit

The rebuild performs no browsing or paid model execution. It replays the public Flyvbjerg commands, checks the workflow after each material stage, validates the resulting ledger, and regenerates the comparison artifacts.

15Use the outside view in the actual decision

Analytical dangerDesign response
Remembering only hits and notorious bombsFreeze membership from provenance and release facts.
Calling box-office gross studio profitName the proxy and use transparent thresholds.
Choosing a favorable budget midpointPreserve both bounds and analyze both.
Letting blockbusters define the typical caseShow ordered values, medians, and empirical quantiles.
Deleting pandemic films after weak outcomesRetain eligible cases; show release period as sensitivity.
Interpreting absent data as failureUse coverage states; missing is never zero.
Calling a seed list a population base rateCarry the enumeration limitation into the decision.

A greenlight model should add distributor rentals, marketing, financing, participations, ancillary revenue, and cash-flow timing. The historical distribution challenges an inside-view forecast; it does not provide audited probabilities of net profit.

Best next step. Audit the cohort against an independent enumeration, then add reported net profit or loss as a separate metric with explicit coverage. Never infer it when a source provides only budget and gross.

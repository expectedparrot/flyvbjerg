# Reference-class definition

## Decision question

What outside-view distribution should a producer or financier use when
evaluating the theatrical economics of a film adaptation of a stage musical?

## Seed cohort

English-language feature films directly adapted from a previously produced
stage musical, released theatrically in the United States from 2006 through
2025 by an established theatrical distributor. Sequels that are not themselves
direct adaptations of the stage work, original screen musicals, animated
films, concert films, television films, and streaming-first releases are
excluded.

Membership is determined from creative provenance and release facts before
budget and box-office outcomes are collected. This documented 18-film seed
cohort was assembled from Wikipedia film histories and is not yet certified as
an exhaustive census.

## Financial outcome

Wikipedia-reported production-budget bounds and worldwide theatrical gross are
recorded in nominal US dollars. No budget midpoint is substituted. The gross
multiple therefore has a conservative bound (`gross / budget_high`) and an
optimistic bound (`gross / budget_low`).

Worldwide gross is not studio revenue or accounting profit. Production budgets
typically omit marketing and distribution, while box-office gross includes the
exhibitor share and omits home entertainment, streaming, television, music,
and other ancillary revenue. The analysis labels economic zones rather than
claiming exact profit:

- below 1.0x: worldwide gross did not cover reported production budget;
- 1.0x to below 2.5x: indeterminate from theatrical gross and budget alone;
- 2.5x or above: strong theatrical-recovery proxy, not verified net profit.

The 2.5x threshold is a scenario convention and is sensitivity-tested at 2.0x.
Reported studio profit or loss estimates, where Wikipedia supplies them, remain
separate descriptive claims and do not overwrite the common metric.

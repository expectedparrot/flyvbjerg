# Pilot reference-class definition

## Question

How long do large technology acquisitions take from definitive agreement
announcement to a terminal state, and what fraction complete within 6, 12, 18,
or 24 months?

## Seed-cohort rule

One publicly announced definitive agreement, announced from 2015 through 2022,
to acquire a company whose principal business is technology, with announced
consideration of at least US$20 billion. Membership is determined from
announcement-era facts. Completed and terminated agreements are both eligible.

Hostile proposals without a definitive agreement are excluded. Transaction
value is used only for selection, not as an outcome. This first pass is a
documented seed cohort assembled from Wikipedia acquisition and company pages;
it is not yet certified exhaustive.

## Cases

- Dell–EMC
- Qualcomm–NXP
- IBM–Red Hat
- AMD–Xilinx
- Salesforce–Slack
- Nvidia–Arm
- Microsoft–Activision Blizzard
- Broadcom–VMware
- Adobe–Figma
- Elon Musk–Twitter

## Outcomes

The terminal state is `completed` or `terminated`. Duration is elapsed calendar
days from public definitive-agreement announcement through completion or
termination. A terminated deal is not missing and is not coded as a zero-day
completion. Pending deals would be right-censored rather than excluded.

## Known selection limitation

The cohort must be checked against an independent enumeration of all qualifying
announcements before its proportions are used as market base rates. The pilot
is sufficient to stress-test target location, thresholds, competing terminal
states, and Wikipedia-based chronology.
